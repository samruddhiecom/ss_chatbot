# Layer A — Planning guidance (DRAFT)
# Neutral, framework-level scaffolding the bot uses to ORGANISE what the founder says.
# Never prescriptive. Mirrors the four stages. Also holds deferral language per boundary topic.
# Chunk convention: ### [layer=A; stage=<idea|market_customer|offer_channels|operations|any>; type=<guidance|deferral_language>]

### [layer=A; stage=idea; type=guidance]
Idea and stage. Aim to capture what the founder is building, the problem it solves, who it is for at a first pass, and how far along they are (just an idea, prototype, first users, first revenue). A good idea framing states the problem, the proposed solution, and the current stage in plain terms. A gap here looks like: no clear problem statement, or a solution described without a problem.

### [layer=A; stage=market_customer; type=guidance]
Market and customer. Capture the founder's own understanding of who the customer is and why they would care. Organise their stated segments, pains, and alternatives. Do not add market data the founder did not provide, and do not estimate market size. A gap looks like: an assumed customer with no evidence of the need, or a market claim that has not been validated with real people.

### [layer=A; stage=offer_channels; type=guidance]
Offer and channels. Capture what exactly is being offered (product, service, pricing shape if stated) and how the founder plans to reach customers. Organise channels the founder named. A gap looks like: an offer with no pricing thinking, or reliance on a single unproven channel.

### [layer=A; stage=operations; type=guidance]
Operations basics. Capture how the thing gets delivered: team, tools, key steps, and any obvious constraints the founder mentions. A gap looks like: no view of who does the work, or a delivery step with a clear single point of failure.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=guidance]
Naming gaps. A gap is anything unknown, unvalidated, or deferred. Every gap should turn into a concrete next step. Prefer validation actions the founder can do quickly (talk to five target customers, run a small test, confirm a price with a real buyer) over abstract advice.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=deferral_language]
Legal topics (entity choice, contracts, IP, LLC vs S-corp). Give brief general framing of what the decision involves, then defer: a licensed professional should weigh in on the specifics before deciding. Record the decision as a named gap with a next step to consult a professional. Return to the current stage.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=deferral_language]
Tax topics (how much tax, deductions, structure for tax). Give brief general framing, then defer: a licensed accountant or tax professional should weigh in on the specifics. Record it as a named gap. Return to the current stage.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=deferral_language]
Financing and lending topics (should I take this loan, financing options). Give brief general framing of the trade-off, then defer to a licensed financial professional for the founder's specific situation. Record it as a named gap. Return to the current stage.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=deferral_language]
Investment and valuation topics (what is my company worth, how much to raise). Give brief general framing, then defer to a qualified professional. Do not produce a valuation figure. Record it as a named gap. Return to the current stage.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=deferral_language]
Projection requests (project my revenue, forecast my growth). Do not produce fabricated forecasts. Explain briefly that credible projections come from the founder's own validated inputs, and record building a grounded model as a named gap and next step. Return to the current stage.

### [layer=A; stage=any; type=deferral_language]
Market statistics requests (market size, TAM, how many customers exist). Do not generate statistics. Explain that market size should be validated with real sources and customer evidence, and record validation as a named gap and next step. Return to the current stage.
